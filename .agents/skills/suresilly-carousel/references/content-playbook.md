## Part 2.5 · Topic selection — the moment, not the concept

The reaction we are aiming for is not "interesting." It is **"this put words to something I
have felt for years and could never say."** That reaction has a precise cause, and it starts
with the topic, before a single line is written.

Our own decks show the failure clearly. `it_was_never_about_the_dishes` works. `atomic_habits_identity`
does not. The difference is not writing quality — it is that one names a scene and the other
names a concept.

### Test 1 · The scene test (the one that matters most)

**State the topic in concrete nouns a camera could film.** If you cannot, you do not have a
topic yet — you have the *explanation* for one. Go find the scene it explains.

| Not a topic (concept) | A topic (scene) |
|---|---|
| anxious attachment | you typed it, deleted it, typed it again |
| emotional labour | you are the only one who knows when the bins go out |
| hypervigilance | rereading a one-word reply, checking the punctuation |
| conflict avoidance | saying sorry when you know you were right, to stop the silence |
| the identity trap | you bought the running shoes. they are still in the box. |

*Dishes* is a filmable object. That is the entire reason that deck outperforms.
Concepts are what you *pay off* on slides 3–7. They are never the way in.

#### Then push it further: the specificity effect

The scene test gets you to a scene. This gets you to **the** scene. Counter-intuitively, **the
weirdly specific line reaches more people than the general one**, because specificity is what
makes the reader believe you have actually lived it:

> Generic: *"If you ever feel anxious when someone takes a while to reply…"*
> Specific: *"If you've ever checked whether they were online, then put your phone face-down
> so you'd stop — this is for you."*

The second one is narrower on paper and lands wider in practice. The detail — *face-down* — is
what makes a stranger think **"this person has been in my head."** General statements get
agreement; specific ones get recognition, and only recognition gets shared.

**Write to one person, never to an audience.** Not *"people who over-explain"* — one person,
mid-scroll, who did the exact thing you are describing this week. Pick the odd, slightly
embarrassing, true detail: the drafted message, the rehearsed phone call, the face-down phone.
If a line could appear in any therapist's post, it is not specific yet.

The risk to watch: specific must stay **common**. A detail so unusual only you have lived it
buys credibility from nobody. Aim for *"I have never said this out loud but yes"* — private,
not rare.

### Test 2 · The self-blame inversion

Every deck that lands takes something the reader privately believes is a **character flaw** and
reattributes it to an **adaptation that once made sense.** Find what they quietly blame
themselves for. That is your topic.

> Reader believes: *"I'm too much. I'm needy."*
> Deck says: *"You check because checking used to be the only warning you got."*

If a topic has no self-blame to invert, it will inform but it will not move anyone. Ask
directly: **what does the reader think is wrong with them here?** No answer means no deck.

### Test 3 · The specificity ladder

Every topic exists at three altitudes. **Always ship the bottom rung.**

```
category      →  behaviour              →  scene  ← ship this
conflict         apologising to end        saying sorry when you know you were
avoidance        arguments                 right, just to make the silence stop
```

If your hook is at category level, you have written a textbook heading.

### Test 4 · The niche fence — SURGE: 70%+ Relational / Attachment

@suresilly is **relational psychology** — how people behave with other people, and with
themselves in relation to other people. For surge (starting off, need followers), **70%+ of decks must be Relational / Attachment** — People-pleasing, Hypervigilance, Family/Trauma, Boundaries, Self-worth. These drive 9.8 shareability and 4-6x shares vs finance (TAM ~2B, attachment +340% since 2020). Adjacent-but-out: productivity, habits, discipline, career, general self-improvement, book summaries, pure general philosophy without a relationship scene.

`atomic_habits_identity` failed this test. It is a competent deck about a good book, and it is
not this page. **A book summary is not an insight.** If a deck could run unchanged on a
productivity account, do not make it. For surge, if your scene could run on a general anxiety page without mentioning another person, it's not relational enough — add the other person.

### Test 5 · Rotate the pattern

Five of our first six decks used **The Hidden Mechanism**. That is how a page starts to feel
same-y even when each post is individually fine. Before writing, check the last five decks in
`carousels/` and pick a pattern none of them used. Tier 1 is ~70% of output *spread across six
patterns*, not 70% on one.

## Part 2.6 · Utility first, hook last

**Order: job → moment → one mechanism → tools → hook.** Hook-first optimises for the swipe and turns the value into insight that nobody acts on. The hook is squeezed out of the tools at the end.

### 1 · Pick the job

The reader hires a carousel at midnight for exactly one of these:

| Job | They are |
|---|---|
| Give me words | at 11pm, unable to form the sentence without rehearsing it four times |
| Give me a tiny next step | frozen in front of an inbox or an appointment |
| Give me proof to send | tired of explaining themselves |
| Give me a way to act *while* anxious | not willing to wait until they feel calm |

**Not hirable for one of these in the next 24 hours? Pick another topic.** Also name the anti-job — what this deck is *not* doing.

### 2 · Lock the moment

One clock time, one place, one body. *"11:47pm, bed, phone, chest tight, left on read."*
If it works for every evening, it works for none.

### 3 · Build the cheat sheet FIRST

Before a word of slide copy. **If you cannot write these, kill the idea now** — this is the entire reason anyone saves a deck.

| Piece | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Script | <20w, copy-paste, with `[brackets]` | *"I'm flooded, need 20 min, back at 7:30"* |
| Intention | `I will [do X] at [time] in [place]` | *"I will set a 10:00 timer at the kitchen table at 3:47"* |
| Habit stack | `After I [existing habit], I will…` | *"After I close the laptop at 11pm, I will open only the one email"* |
| If-then | `If [trigger], then [2-min response]` | Must work while anxious. No googling. One behaviour. |

### 4 · The three beats

1. **Name the scene before explaining it.** Slides 1–2 are pure scene. No diagnosis before slide 3.
2. **Invert the blame, then bridge to the tool.** *"You learned this where moods changed without warning"* → *"so here is what you can say"*. Reassurance soothes; explanation plus a tool redirects.
3. **Give words and a tiny next step they can steal.** One script and one if-then, minimum.

### 5 · The gate — abort if any fails

| Check | Fails if |
|---|---|
| 24 hours, no googling | It needs searching, inventing, or motivation |
| Words, not why | Fewer than 2 slides are copy-paste scripts |
| One moment | Slide 1 and slides 7–8 do not name the same scene |
| Screenshot save | Slide 8 is a summary rather than a self-contained tool |

One behaviour at a time. Targeting several is how implementation intentions fail.

### Hook craft — the 12 rules for slide 1

A hook fails by being **long and abstract**, not by being badly written. Every rule below was broken by a shipped deck.

| # | Rule | Do this, not that |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **≤8 words, 12 hard max.** Length is an intent switch, not a moral. | 8w for sends (swipe + DM). 9–12w only if you can say *"this deck is for saves"*. |
| 2 | **No second-person diagnosis.** Clinical nouns are fine; labelling the reader is not. | `You rehearsed a text for 2 minutes` · not `You have rejection sensitivity` |
| 3 | **Filmable or fail.** A body, number, time or place a camera could see. | `you reread 'okay' 4 times` · not `anxiety in communication` |
| 4 | **Second person, specific.** Write to one person mid-scroll who did this *this week*. | `you` beats `people` beats `we` |
| 5 | **Leave the loop open.** Do not answer the hook in its own second sentence. | `It was never about the dishes.` · not `…It was about being unseen.` |
| 6 | **Stage the scene, do not announce the lesson.** | `You typed it, deleted it, typed it again` · not `How to stop over-explaining` |
| 7 | **H2 is an aside, ≤7 words.** If it carries meaning H1 needed, H1 is wrong. | Fix H1, delete H2 |
| 8 | **H1 mirrors, H2 absolves.** H1 names the hidden behaviour; H2 removes the shame. | `You check your phone 58 times before getting out of bed` / `You're not undisciplined — you're scanning for safety` |
| 9 | **First three words carry it.** Openers that delay the noun are deletable. | Cut `Why`, `The reason`, `Here's how`, `What nobody tells you about` |
| 10 | **Read it aloud.** One breath, or cut it. | — |
| 11 | **Slide 2 is a second cover.** ≤25 words, its own `[[accent]]`. | Never open it with "Let me explain" |
| 12 | **Re-hook on slides 4 and 6.** 6–10 words, in body copy. | Never write "Swipe →" — the renderer adds it |

**Banned openers, enforced in code:** `Why` · `How to` · `The reason` · `What nobody` · `Most people` · `Here's`.
**Also banned:** em dashes in the hook, and the `not X, it's Y` seesaw.

**Timeframes are mostly not ours.** *"How I fixed my attachment style in 30 days"* is a transformation promise we cannot keep, from exactly the guru posture we avoid. Tier 2 move, use sparingly.

#### Fixing our own weak hooks

Every "before" here shipped. This is the exact failure mode to avoid.

| Before | Why it drags | After |
|---|---|---|
| Why you can tell someone is mad before they even know they're mad *(12w)* | Three words of scaffolding, then a clause that doubles back on itself | **You knew before they did.** *(5w)* |
| Why calm people make your nervous system panic *(8w)* | Diagnosis language — "nervous system" is a slide-3 word | **Calm people make you nervous.** *(5w)* |
| You don't lack discipline. You have an identity trap. *(9w)* | Two sentences; the second one explains and kills the gap | **You don't lack discipline.** *(4w)* |
| When your partner starts shutting down *(6w)* | Already works — concrete, second person, one beat | *keep* |

### Slide 3's source — you do not pick it

**Do not choose a citation from a list, and never type an author, title or year onto a slide.** That is invariant 12.

`bibliography.py` finds and proves the citation for every deck. Five gates, all fail closed:

1. The book exists in Open Library **and is shelved there as psychology**
2. The claim carries no unverifiable statistic
3. The term of art appears in scanned text
4. A model that did **not** propose it fails to refute it
5. Code assembles the line from the catalogue's own spelling

Whatever survives is appended to `references/citations.json` — a record of what has been proved, not a list anybody maintains.

A model may *suggest* a book. It may never name one on a slide.
