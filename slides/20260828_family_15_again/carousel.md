## Caption

You're a grown adult.
You pay rent, handle emergencies, and manage projects.
Then you step through your parents' front door.
And within seven minutes, you're 15 again.

Why do family visits feel like an emotional time machine?

In family systems theory, families operate like emotional ecosystems that strive to stay predictable. When you return to the environment where your original role was formed, the sensory cues—the kitchen smell, the tone of voice, the old seating arrangement—trigger your nervous system to load its teenage survival script.

Regression isn't proof that your personal growth is fake. It's just context memory firing old habits.

Next time you feel the teenage urge to slam a door:
1. Don't fight to prove you've changed.
2. Use the physical reset: step outside for 10 minutes.
3. Use the adult script from Slide 5: "I appreciate the suggestion, but I'm handling this my way."

Save slide 8 before your next trip home.

Send this to the sibling who shares your family group chat side-eyes.
